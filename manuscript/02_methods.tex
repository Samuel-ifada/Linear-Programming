\section{Methods}
\subsection{Linear Programming Framework}
Linear programming (LP) provides a framework for solving optimization and
feasibility problems subject to linear constraints. In the present work, LP is
used to reformulate activation screening as a feasibility problem in composition
space. Rather than generating candidate alloy compositions and evaluating whether
they satisfy prescribed activation criteria, the screening criteria are expressed
directly as linear constraints on alloy composition. The set of compositions
satisfying all constraints defines a feasible region within the atomic-fraction
simplex.

Because the FLARE activation model is linear in alloy composition, the resulting
feasible region forms a convex polytope that can be represented analytically
through a system of linear inequalities. This representation enables the geometry
of the feasible space to be characterized and explored without evaluating
individual compositions during the screening stage. Feasible alloy compositions
are subsequently obtained through LP-guided enumeration of the admissible region,
thereby replacing the conventional generate-and-filter workflow with a
constraint-first screening methodology.
